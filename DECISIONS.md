# Decisions

Where the spec says nothing, I pick the simpler option and log it here. Changes to what the spec says are in `PLAN.md` section 2, where approving the plan approves them. Newest at the bottom.

| # | Area | Decision | Reason |
|---|------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Units | One world unit is one centimeter. 100% zoom draws 1 cm as one screen pixel. | Matches the spec's storage unit. A 30 m room is 3,000 px wide at 100%, so "fit to content" lands near 40%. |
| 2 | Units | Feet show as feet and inches to the nearest inch (`5′ 6″`), meters to two decimals, areas as whole square feet or m² to one decimal. | Easy to read; the spec gives no rounding. |
| 3 | Geometry | `x` and `y` on every item mean its center. Rotation is in degrees, clockwise, about the center. | Rotation and resizing about the center need no extra math. |
| 4 | Seats | Seat circles are 45 cm across and sit 8 cm out from the table edge. | Close to a real chair; the spec needs one size everywhere. |
| 5 | Seats | One-sided tables put their seats along the back edge (local top), facing the room. Rotate the table to aim it. | The spec says "facing the room" with no room direction to read. |
| 6 | Seats | A 2-seat round table places both seats side by side on the back arc. | The spec's sweetheart preset is a 36 in round with 2 seats side by side. |
| 7 | Seats | The rect preset (8 ft by 30 in, 8 seats) seats the two long sides only: 4 on each. The square preset seats all four sides, 2 per side. | Gives the spec's seat counts at 24 in per seat. |
| 8 | Seats | On tables seated on more than one side, each seat in turn goes to the side where it gets the most edge. | Unlike sharing by length with the largest remainder, this never crowds a short end, stays stable when + and − add or take one seat, and makes a table tight exactly when its count is over Fit seats. |
| 9 | Seats | + adds a seat to the side with the most spare edge; − takes an empty seat and leaves everyone else in place. When the count, size or sides change, guests keep their order around the table (see 63). | Nobody jumps across the table when the seats re-space. |
| 10 | Seats | When a count change would unseat people, the guests in the highest seat numbers go, and the dialog names them first. | Predictable, and you see who moves. |
| 11 | Tables | Minimum size 24 in (60.96 cm) on every side. Round tables keep width equal to height through every resize. Seat limits: 30 on round and square tables, 60 on rectangles. | Spec, plus a cap so the + button cannot add more seats than any table could hold. |
| 12 | Rules | Seated guests are checked whatever their RSVP; a seated guest who answered no also gets a warning. Unseated guests never break a rule. | One plain rule, and the warning catches the real mistake. |
| 13 | Rules | A rule applies to a layout when its `appliesTo` is `both` or matches the layout's kind. Couple rules apply to both kinds. | Spec field, made explicit; couple rules have no row to set it on. |
| 14 | Problems | A wall through a table counts as a problem. Tight tables, narrow gaps and seated guests who answered no are warnings. Only tables count as neighbors for the gap check; touching tables show "overlapping". | PLAN change 16. |
| 15 | Walls | Wall crossings are worked out when a layout opens and after each move, from the template's walls and the layout's tables. Nothing is stored. | Templates can change at any time, and a stored flag would soon be wrong. |
| 16 | Editor | Zoom, pan and the layer switches are kept per device. Selection, tool and hover are never kept. | PLAN change 1. |
| 17 | Editor | Undo covers canvas edits only, seating included. Guest, group and rule edits save at once, one row at a time, outside the undo history. | They are stored outside the layout. |
| 18 | Editor | Loading the other person's version clears this tab's undo history. | Undo would otherwise put back a snapshot that wipes their work. |
| 19 | Mode | Edit or View is remembered per device. The first visit picks View on phones and touch-only devices or under 768 px wide, else Edit. | Spec, plus PLAN change 13. |
| 20 | Author | The Noa / Aiden switch is remembered per device. The first visit asks. Replaced by 118: who edited is the signed-in account. | No accounts in v1. |
| 21 | Guests | Seat labels use first name and last name. Nicknames show in search and the hover card. | Default for open question Q2. |
| 22 | Guests | The side list's counts ("142 guests · 118 seated · 24 unassigned") count guests who said yes or have not answered; declined guests show as a separate number. | A declined guest needs no seat. |
| 23 | Search | Clicking a seated name zooms to at least close level before the seat pulses. | You can then read the names around them. |
| 24 | Import | Unknown CSV columns go into notes as `column: value` lines, once. | The spec says "into notes"; this keeps the column name. |
| 25 | Paste | A comma means "Last, First". Otherwise the last word is the last name, unless a particle (van, von, de, del, da, di, du, la, le, al, bin, ben) starts it earlier. Honorifics are dropped. Lines holding "&" or "and" are flagged in the preview for you to split. | Covers the usual cases; you fix the rest in the preview. |
| 26 | Share | A share link always shows the layout as last saved. | The spec's "latest version". |
| 27 | Share | Each share link has a label (Caterer, DJ) for the Links list. | Hard to tell links apart otherwise. |
| 28 | Templates | Template saves raise a version and use the same conflict check and saved-twice protection as layouts. | The template editor saves the same way as the layout editor. |
| 29 | Templates | A layout whose template was deleted opens with a notice and no venue layer; its settings let you pick another template. | Restoring a wedding in the database can bring back such a layout. |
| 30 | Delete | Deleting a layout leaves its share links in the database, but they stop working. | Undoing the layout delete in the database brings the links back. |
| 31 | Delete | Weddings, layouts, templates and guests are marked deleted and kept. Groups and rules are deleted outright. | The spec asks for the first three to be kept. Guests carry group and rule links worth getting back; their seats and partner link are cleared on removal (PLAN change 7). |
| 32 | Delete | Deleting a wedding marks only the wedding row. | Every query skips rows whose wedding is deleted, so clearing that one field brings everything back. |
| 33 | API | API routes reply 401 without a valid cookie; pages go to `/login`. | A redirect means nothing to `fetch`. |
| 34 | Ceremony | After the seating block tool places rows, each row is its own item. "Select block" picks them all; "Add row behind" adds one. | Rows stay simple items, and the spec wants each row editable on its own. |
| 35 | Grid | When grid lines would sit closer than 8 px on screen, only every fifth (feet) or every second (meters) line shows. | Dense lines turn the canvas grey and hide the plan. |
| 36 | Runtime | Node 22 (`engines: 22.x`, so Vercel never moves to a newer major on its own). npm as the package manager. | Matches this sandbox and pdf.js's minimum. pnpm blocks install scripts by default, which can break packages that build native code on Vercel. |
| 37 | Branches | Each milestone is built on its own branch and merged into `main` when its tests pass. | Vercel deploys `main` to the live site (PLAN change 29). |
| 38 | Next.js | Cache Components and partial prefetching stay off. | Every page sits behind the password and reads fresh data, so there is nothing to cache, and leaving them off keeps the usual dynamic rendering. |
| 39 | Tests | `@playwright/test` is pinned to 1.63.0, the version whose Chromium is installed in this sandbox. | A newer version would look for a browser that is not there. CI installs the matching browser itself. |
| 40 | Templates | The empty Toronto Beach Club template comes from a database migration. | Production gets it on the first deploy, and every wedding sees it. Replaced in part by 130: it is in the shared library, which every planner sees. |
| 41 | Auth | Cookie names are `sp_site` and `sp_share_<linkId>`. The logout route needs the site cookie, like every other route. Replaced by 120: the site cookie is gone (only its name is kept, to clear one a browser still holds); the cookies are `sp_session` and `sp_share_<linkId>`, and logout still needs a session. | Otherwise another site could sign you out with a hidden form. A signed-out tab still lands on the login page. |
| 42 | Files | The env template is `env.example`, without the leading dot. | This environment's security rules block any file named `.env*`, even one with blank values. |
| 43 | Auth | The proxy sends signed-out visitors to `/login` and adds `?next=` for any page other than home. | Returns them to the page they asked for. |
| 44 | Crawlers | `robots.txt` lets crawlers in; every response carries `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`. | A crawler kept out by `robots.txt` never sees the noindex header and can still list the bare URL. |
| 45 | Auth | A production build fails, naming the setting, when `SITE_PASSWORD`, `AUTH_SECRET` (32+ characters) or `DATABASE_URL` is missing. Replaced by 137: `SITE_PASSWORD` is no longer checked, and the backup settings are. | Otherwise the site deploys and then fails at sign-in with no clue why. |
| 46 | Auth | When a login block starts or ends, its count starts again from zero. | Without this, attempts during a block kept renewing it for up to an hour, longer than the 15 minutes promised. |
| 47 | Editor | The editor's document store is a small store read with React's `useSyncExternalStore`; Zustand is not installed (PLAN section 3 named it). | The store needs only get, set and subscribe, and one less dependency. Undo uses `src/domain/history.ts` either way. |
| 48 | Author | The "Who is using this device?" prompt sits in a corner and does not block the page. Editing, Duplicate and New layout wait until someone picks, with a short note saying why. Replaced by 118: nothing waits. | Reading needs no author; every save records one. |
| 49 | Saving | Renaming a layout or changing its template leaves its version and "last edited" time alone. "Last edited" follows canvas saves. | A rename is not an edit to the plan, and must not cause a save conflict. |
| 50 | Saving | Leaving the editor inside the app sends waiting edits first, waiting up to 3 seconds. | The layouts list then shows the latest thumbnail and time. |
| 51 | Saving | The server keeps the ids of the 20 most recent saves of each layout. A retried save that landed earlier gets "already saved" with that save's version, and a reopened tab uses the list to tell that its last save landed. | One remembered id was not enough: once anyone saved after it, a landed save looked lost and showed a false conflict, which iPhones hit when a tab is closed from the switcher. |
| 52 | Text | Free text in layouts and names has NUL characters removed and broken emoji halves repaired before saving. | Postgres refuses both, and the save would fail and retry forever. |
| 53 | API | Ids that are not uuids get 404. Lists reply with plain arrays. Creates reply 201 with the full object. | Plain, predictable replies. |
| 54 | Seed | `npm run seed` runs tsx with the `react-server` condition, so it writes layouts through the app's own `writeCanvas()`. | Every canvas write goes through one function (PLAN section 5). |
| 55 | Recovery | After a 410 (layout deleted elsewhere) or 422, the editor offers "Save as a copy" (a new layout in the same wedding) before "Download a copy". | Unsaved work stays in the app. |
| 56 | Editor | Locked items cannot be moved, resized, turned or deleted; their seats, names and labels can still change. Copies of locked items come out unlocked. | Lock guards against stray drags, not against seating. |
| 57 | Touch | On touch, a quick one-finger drag pans unless it starts on a handle or an already selected item; holding 450 ms and then dragging moves an item. A press must move 8 px to count as a drag (4 px with a mouse). | Panning is the common touch action, and fingers wobble more than mice. |
| 58 | Editor | A selection box picks up unlocked items only. With the phone Select switch on, a tap adds an item to the selection or takes it out. | Locked items are meant to stay put; phones have no Shift. |
| 59 | Editor | Paste in the same layout lands one grid step past the last copy; in another layout it lands in the middle of the view, or where you pressed when pasting from the canvas menu. | Copies never hide exactly under their originals. |
| 60 | Camera | Fit to content stops at 200%, and frames the venue's walls when the venue layer shows. | One small table should not fill the screen, and a room should be seen whole. |
| 61 | Spacing | The gap check measures between table edges (no chairs), as the spec says. Guides, hit tests and fit use the footprint with chairs. | Tables 5 ft apart at their edges are normal and must not warn. |
| 62 | Snapping | Resizing snaps the size to whole grid steps in the table's own frame; moving snaps a table by its body. Alt turns snapping off. | Standard sizes (36, 48, 60, 72 in) stay reachable wherever the table sits. |
| 63 | Seats | Changing the seat count keeps guests in the same order around the table; − takes the empty seat out of the order and leaves everyone else in place. | Nobody changes side or neighbours, which the together rules depend on. |
| 64 | Tables | Changing a table's shape is refused when it has more seats than the new shape allows; a table turned round seats all the way around (2-seat rounds keep the side-by-side arc). | Nobody is unseated by a shape change. |
| 65 | Mouse | The mouse-or-trackpad guess treats wheel steps of 50 px or more as a mouse notch and events under 250 ms apart as one gesture. | A guess; the "Mouse wheel: zoom / pan" setting covers devices it gets wrong. |
| 66 | Speed | The grid's CSS variables are registered as not inherited. | Otherwise every pan frame restyled every element in the canvas and phones dropped frames. |
| 67 | Tables | A table may store which side gives up a seat first (`fewerSeatsSide`), so − on a no-ends table takes the seat from the side that has the empty one. Older layouts without it still load. | Without it the seat count alone decides the sides, and a guest would have to move round the end. |
| 68 | Tables | Tables are at most 120 ft (3,657.6 cm) on a side, enough for 60 seats along one edge. | Stops a Shift+drag from making a table the save would refuse. |
| 69 | Editor | A 44 px More button sits beside every selection in Edit mode and opens the same menu as right click or a long press. | PLAN change 14; phones need a visible way to the menu. |
| 70 | Guests | The guest CSV export adds a `nickname` column after `guest_id`, and the import reads a Nickname column (nickname, preferred name, goes by, known as) the way it reads other fields: a blank cell leaves it as it was. | Search matches nicknames, so an export that dropped them lost data. The sample file keeps the spec's ten columns. |
| 71 | Groups | Group names cannot hold `;`. A guest's lone group whose name holds a comma is written to the CSV with a trailing `;`. | The groups cell splits on `;`, or on `,` when it has none, so either would come back from a re-import as two groups. |
| 72 | Import | A file that is not UTF-8 is read as Windows-1252 or Mac Roman, whichever reads more like names (a tie stays Windows-1252). The column step says the file is not UTF-8 and offers the other one. | Excel for Mac's "CSV (Macintosh)" writes Mac Roman, which puts accented letters on other bytes; read as Windows-1252, "Renée" becomes "RenŽe" and no longer matches. |
| 73 | Text | Guest, group and import text is stored in NFC. | A name typed or pasted in decomposed form (e plus a combining mark) would otherwise differ from the same name read from a file, and every re-import would report it changed. |
| 74 | Seating | In View mode, or before the page can edit, a name in the side list never starts a drag: a press let go within 8 px is a tap however long it was held, and a seated name pans to its seat. Keyboard activation of an unassigned name does nothing in View mode. | Phones open in View mode, and a slow tap must still pan (spec section 6) instead of showing "Switch to Edit". |
| 75 | Touch | A second finger during a one-finger guest drag cancels the drag; other drags still end where they are. | A guest is dropped only where the finger lifts. Pinching to reach another table must not drop or swap anyone. |
| 76 | Seating | A drop swaps with a taken seat only inside its circle (up to 4 px past its edge, at most a quarter of its radius). Elsewhere on a table's top or chair ring a drop takes the first free seat. The 22 px reach round small seats applies to empty seats only, just outside the chair ring. | Zoomed out, 22 px round a taken seat covers most of a table, and a drop meant for the table would send someone to Unassigned. |
| 77 | Seating | The + sits in every empty seat, drawn in the world SVG and shown by CSS in Edit mode at the middle and close levels; presses find it from the seat geometry. It never takes a press that lands on another item. | No cap on how many seats get one, nothing to move per camera frame, and a neighbouring fixture or table keeps its presses. |
| 78 | Labels | Guest names (close level) and initials (middle level) sit in their own layer above the furniture. Names are worked out when the camera settles at close level, when the plan changes (after a drag) and when the guest list arrives. | Showing, hiding or working them out again then changes nothing inside the furniture layer, which the speed test checks. |
| 79 | Labels | A name that does not fit at full size becomes first name and last initial, shrunk down to 10 px, before it becomes initials. | The spec's order (section 4, "Detail levels"); PLAN section 6 now says the same. |
| 80 | Rules | An unseated guest who answered no is left out of a rule. A rule left with fewer than two guests that way reads as ok (or not seated); "needs guests" stays for rules whose guests were removed. | Otherwise a declined partner leaves a couple rule unfinished forever, and a declined guest's place in a next-to run hides a real gap. |
| 81 | Seating | The whole right panel, whichever tab is open, and the phone's sheet count as the side list when a seated guest is dragged back to unseat them. | A bigger target, and it works from the Properties tab too. |
| 82 | Seating | In milestone 5 the guest menu offers Unseat; Add to group, Keep together and Keep apart come with milestone 6. Properties names selected guests and offers Unseat too. | Groups and rules get their editor tools in milestone 6. |
| 83 | Speed | Only the label set for the current level is in the page, and none while a gesture or a glide zooms the view: names and initials leave when a zoom starts and fade back in (120 ms) when the camera settles. Panning keeps them. A level change re-renders only the names layer. | Chrome lays every SVG text out again at each new scale, even text hidden with display: none. With 400 names and 400 initials in the page the speed test dropped 40% of frames with the CPU slowed 4x; without them, none. PLAN section 6 had CSS fade between three sets kept in the page. |
| 84 | Rules | Keep together and Keep apart in the editor's guest menu save a new rule at once with the defaults (not next to each other, both layout kinds, on); the Rules page edits it. | 88 | Ceremony | In Edit mode a chair's + opens the guest picker only once its row is selected; the first click or tap on a row selects it. | A row is all chairs with no body to click, so without this an empty row could not be picked to label, reserve or change. |
| 89 | Notes | Adding a note needs Noa or Aiden picked on the device. With nobody picked, the note tools are refused with "Pick Noa or Aiden at the top first." Replaced by 118: a note records the signed-in user's name and account id. | Each note records who wrote it (spec section 9). |

| 92 | Arrows | An attached end is not stored. Each time the plan draws, it sits where the line towards the other end leaves the item's outline, chairs included. The stored point is a fallback, and deleting the item leaves the end there as a free point. | A table that moves, turns or resizes needs no arrow edit, so undo and save conflicts stay simple. |
| 93 | Arrows | Arrows and lines change by their two end handles only; they never turn or take a size. Both ends cannot hold the same item. Dragging an arrow held at both ends by items that stay put moves nothing. A copied arrow lets go of items not copied with it. | Keeps arrow geometry plain. |
| 94 | Notes | Sticky and text notes are typed into in place. Enter, or a second click or tap on the selected note, starts typing; Esc, Cmd/Ctrl+Enter, a press elsewhere or switching to View saves the text as one undo step. | 97 | Restore | Restore sends the wedding's rows, then each venue template followed by its floor plan image, then each layout (PLAN section 7 listed layouts before templates). | Each layout then points at its restored template from its first write, so no step has to go back and fix layouts. |

| 117 | Editor | A right press that moves more than 4 px pans, on items too and in both modes; one that moves less is a right click and opens the menu. | 118 | Accounts | Who made a change is the signed-in account, from the session: saves, new layouts, copies and venue writes record the user's id and display name, and no request body names the editor (an old tab's `editedBy` is ignored). Notes keep the maker's name and id, set by the browser. Words compare account ids, not names ("You changed this layout in another tab or on another device"). Unsaved edits live in each user's own IndexedDB databases; the old shared ones move into the first user to sign in on the device. A save refused because someone else signed in on the browser (401 stale_page) stops for good in that tab and keeps the draft for its own user. Log out sends what waits (all of the user's tabs, up to 3 s), asks before dropping anything unsaved, then deletes the user's databases and clipboard. | A shared device must never send one person's edits under another's account, nor show them to the next person. |
| 119 | Accounts | People sign in with an email and a password (10 to 200 characters, stored as argon2id hashes). There is no open sign-up: an account comes from a one-time invite link an owner or an admin made, or, for the first one, from `/setup`. The app sends no email; whoever makes a link copies it and sends it by hand. Accounts are turned off (and back on) by an admin, never deleted, so what they edited stays attributed. | The site holds the guest lists of named people, so only people the owners chose should reach it, and a mail service would be one more thing to run, pay for and secure. |
| 120 | Sessions | The browser holds a cookie (`sp_session`) with a random secret signed under a key made from `AUTH_SECRET`. The database keeps only the SHA-256 hash of that secret, a user agent cut to 200 characters and no IP address. A session lasts 30 days from its last use (written at most every 10 minutes) and never more than 365 days from sign-in. Changing the password, Sign out everywhere and a reset link end sessions in the database at once, and turning an account off makes every request of its sessions answer 401. The cookie is HttpOnly, Secure and SameSite Lax. | A session the server can end is the point of moving off a shared password.
